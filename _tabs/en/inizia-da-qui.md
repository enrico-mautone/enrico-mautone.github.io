If this is your first time here, here's how to find your way around.

This blog is about what happens when you try to build an AI product. I write it while I'm doing it, as CTO of [Stories](https://stories-app.it): that's why you'll find more open questions than final answers in it, and a few mistakes told without beating around the bush.

## Where to begin

- **[The orchestrator, not the coder](/posts/the-orchestrator-not-the-coder/)** — the starting point: what changes in the job of building software when AI agents become the ones doing the work, and judgment is what's left to us.
- **[Ai sei fantastica!!! Si ma quanto mi costi!?!?](/posts/ai-fantastica-ma-quanto-mi-costi/)** *(in Italian)* — the bill that arrives after the excitement, and why you should do the math while the product is still on a slide.
- **[Ma tu quanto sei maturo nell'AI Engineering](/posts/ma-tu-quanto-sei-maturo/)** *(in Italian)* — I measured how well my way of working with AI holds up without me sitting in front of it. Spoiler: level 2.

## The three sections

- **[The trenches](/categories/cantiere/)** — how an AI product is built, and what breaks when you really build it.
- **[The yardstick](/categories/il-metro/)** — how you measure whether an AI system works, when there's no single right answer to compare against.
- **[At the helm](/categories/il-timone/)** — costs, privacy, rules and people: the decisions that stay with whoever is steering, however good the agent is.
